Designing for how Bhubaneswar actually lives

A flat in Patia and a flat in Old Town need different answers. Newer towers off the Infocity and Chandrasekharpur belt come with generous glazing and thin walls — great light, poor sound, and balconies that bake from March. Older builds around Saheed Nagar and Unit blocks have solid masonry, smaller windows and awkward beam drops. Coastal humidity is the constant: it decides which core material goes near the sink, whether a wardrobe back needs a moisture barrier, and how long a cheap laminate lasts.

We start every project with a site survey, not a catalogue. Measurements, light at two times of day, beam and duct positions, water pressure, and a straight conversation about how your family uses the home — joint or nuclear, how often you host, where the pooja space belongs, whether monsoon drying happens on the balcony or indoors.

How we work

  1. Free consultation and site visit. We measure, photograph and listen. No charge, no obligation.
  2. Layout and itemised budget. You see the plan and the numbers together, line by line.
  3. Photoreal 3D. Room-wise views with two revision rounds, so you approve the real thing.
  4. Execution on a fixed schedule. Weekly written updates against a week-by-week plan.
  5. Handover. Styling, deep clean, snag list closed, and a workmanship warranty in writing.

Hiring an interior designer in Bhubaneswar

Ask for three things before you sign anything: a site visit and measurement before any quotation, an itemised budget rather than a lump sum, and 3D views of your own home rather than portfolio images. A designer who will not put the scope in writing is the main source of cost overruns.
