False ceiling and lighting design in Bhubaneswar

Most Indian homes are lit by one tube light per room. Layered light is the cheapest upgrade there is — and the one people notice first.

Book a free consultation Get an instant estimate

Light in three layers

A room needs three kinds of light and most have only one. Ambient fills the room. Task puts light where you read, cook or work. Accent picks out a wall, a niche or a piece of art. Once all three exist and can be switched separately, the same room can be bright at 7 pm and calm at 10.

The false ceiling is mostly a means to that end — it hides the wiring, the cove and the AC ducting, and it lets us drop the light exactly where it should be.

Watch the ceiling height

Most Bhubaneswar flats have a 10-foot slab. A full-room false ceiling drops that by 8–10 inches and can make a compact bedroom feel low. In smaller rooms we usually run a peripheral band with a cove and leave the centre open — you get the light, and you keep the height.

Good to know

Questions about false ceiling & lighting

POP typically runs ₹70–110 per sq.ft and gypsum ₹90–150 per sq.ft, rising to ₹140–260 with cove and profile detailing. Light fixtures and wiring are quoted separately.
Gypsum is faster, flatter and less prone to hairline cracks, so it is the better default. POP is still useful for curved and moulded shapes, and it is slightly cheaper.
A full-room ceiling drops height by 8–10 inches. In rooms under about 11 feet wide we recommend a peripheral band with a cove instead, which gives you the lighting without losing the ceiling height.
Warm white (2700–3000K) in living rooms and bedrooms, neutral white (4000K) over kitchen counters and study desks. Mixing temperatures in the same room is the most common mistake.
