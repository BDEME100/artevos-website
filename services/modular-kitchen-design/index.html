Modular kitchen design in Bhubaneswar

An Indian kitchen takes daily heat, steam, spice and scrubbing. We design for that first, and for looks second — which is why they still look good in year five.

Built for the way Indian kitchens are used

A kitchen that works for daily tadka, pressure cooking and washing at the sink needs different decisions from a showroom display: a moisture-resistant core near the sink, a chimney sized for real smoke, drawers instead of deep shutters for utensils, and a counter height matched to whoever cooks most.

We start with the work triangle — sink, hob, fridge — then place storage where your hands already reach. Only after that do we choose shutters and colours.

What's included

Ergonomic layoutL, U, parallel, straight or island — chosen from your room's plumbing, window and door positions.
Base, wall & tall unitsDrawers for utensils, pull-outs for bottles and spices, a tall unit for the pantry or oven.
Core & shutter selectionBWP ply or HDHMR cores; laminate, acrylic, PU or membrane shutters, with pros and cons explained.
Counter & backsplashGranite, quartz or engineered stone with the right edge profile, plus a tile or glass backsplash.
Appliance integrationChimney, hob, oven, microwave, dishwasher and RO planned into the joinery and the electrical layout.
Lighting & electricalsUnder-cabinet task lighting and enough correctly-placed sockets — the thing most kitchens get wrong.

Materials, in plain language

  • BWP plywood — boiling-water-proof; the safest core for the sink and hob run.
  • HDHMR — dense and stable, takes membrane and PU finishes beautifully; good for shutters.
  • Laminate — hardest-wearing budget finish, huge choice, easy to clean.
  • Acrylic — high gloss, scratch-resistant, shows fingerprints; best on wall units.
  • PU paint — the premium matte look; repairable, but needs a good workshop.

Hardware matters more than the shutter. Soft-close hinges and full-extension channels are the difference between a kitchen that feels solid in year five and one that sags.

How long it takes

Design and 3D take about a week once we have measurements. Factory production plus installation is typically 4–7 weeks. If the kitchen is part of a full-home project we sequence it so plumbing and tiling finish before the units arrive.

Questions about modular kitchens

Cabinetry runs roughly ₹1,600 to ₹5,500 per running foot depending on core, shutters and hardware. A typical 10-foot kitchen lands between ₹1.6 lakh and ₹7 lakh. Counter, backsplash and appliances are quoted separately.
A carpenter can be cheaper up front and is flexible on odd sizes. Modular gives you factory-cut accuracy, better hardware, an actual warranty and far less site dust. For kitchens we recommend modular for the wet zone at minimum.
BWP (boiling-water-proof) plywood for anything near the sink and hob, and HDHMR or BWP for the rest. Avoid plain MDF for base units in a coastal-humid climate.
